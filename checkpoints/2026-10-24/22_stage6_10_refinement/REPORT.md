## Parent-model distinctions that matter

1. Alston, Freebairn and James (2001), AJAE 83:888–902, DOI 10.1111/0002-9092.00217: noncooperative commodity-group advertising shifts rents among related producers, compared with coordinated producer profits. Thus noncooperative excess investment and producer displacement are prior art. The focal government objective here instead includes local producer income plus an equal share of *national consumer surplus*, and the national counterfactual counts all producer and consumer welfare. This is a real welfare-accounting and decision-rights change, but alone not proof of novel economics.
2. Balagtas and Kim (2007), AJAE 89:932–946, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8276.2007.01037.x: competitive reallocation of common raw milk across dairy product markets and associated promotion measurement already exist. Hence endogenous reallocation of a fixed productive resource is not new. Here public quality R&D is selected simultaneously by territorial authorities before farmers' old/new land allocations, and a global Nash/planner contrast is proved rather than a multi-market advertising return measurement.
3. Lence, Marette, Hayes and Foster (2007), AJAE 89:947–963, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8276.2007.01036.x: new geographically differentiated agricultural products, supply choices and collective marketing are already established. Their central institutional comparison concerns producer organizations, supply control, and costs of developing/marketing a differentiated product. Here continuous public research has no entry fee, growers are price takers, and governments choose quality with national consumer-surplus incidence. The verified 2006 working paper propositions 1–3 do not directly provide the present exact two-region public-laboratory Nash/global-national-optimum result; whether the final 2007 paper or a more general parent theorem subsumes it remains unproven.
4. Dixit and Norman (1978), advertising and welfare, and Cardon and Pope (2003), JARE 28:185–200, already establish that privately attractive promotional spending can be socially excessive or have a socially optimal zero. Therefore the sign headline is NOT an independent new theorem.

## Economic novelty: what the altered setup *actually delivers*

A. **Distortion-location separation:** Conditional on public research x, price-taking farmer adoption satisfies exactly the national constrained KKT, even at corners. Nevertheless the preceding decentralized quality-funding choice can be nationally inefficient. This is not a novel welfare theorem in isolation; it is an institutionally useful separation of where the market is efficient from where the public intervention fails. A reviewer can reject a narrative that misattributes the welfare loss to farmers' adoption frictions.

B. **Adoption-composition-dependent research incentive:** For symmetric interior linear demand, the local minus national research marginal incentive is xi(a_star-a), xi=(2h-e)/(2L), a_star=(h-j)/(2h-e). It can change sign as old/new adoption composition varies; the equilibrium effort ranking requires additional globality/feasibility conditions. The restricted nonlinear class has wedge proportional to H_phi(1-a)-H_phi(a), H_phi(q)=q phi''(q). It can be neutral despite nonzero induced planting response, and half-share neutrality disappears under asymmetric old/new curvature. This yields a falsifiable *incidence restriction* specific to the structure, not a universal nonlinear-demand theorem or a previously unknown envelope identity.

C. **Globally harmful public breeding without private market power or fixed fee:** For t=1/2,b=c=n=1/5,k0=kappa=1,R=2,Delta=1/2+z and z in ((-4+sqrt(13))/12,0], the unique Nash has xD=(1+3z)/13>0, aD=(1+16z)/26>0 while the unique global national optimum is xP=aP=0 and WD-WP=(10z-1)(22z+3)/338<0. This uses competitive growers, continuous convex effort cost, quality entering genuine utility, and complete 3-branch unilateral deviation proof; no advertising monopoly, patent prize or fixed entry cost is required. It is a new **proved model-specific characterization**, not a claim that negative welfare from demand-shifting investment is first discovered here. In this analytic family e=j=0, so no cross-government strategic interaction; an e=1/100 exact rational example independently supports nonzero strategic-substitute coupling, but not an analytic open neighborhood or uniqueness of all asymmetric Nash equilibria.

## Why this may matter economically

A regional government can correctly fund quality improvement for its producers and yet reduce nationwide welfare through displaced market rents, even though farmers' subsequent land reallocation is conditionally efficient. The research distortion is not mechanically proportional to R&D spending or market power; it depends on old/new composition and, in the restricted nonlinear extension, demand curvature. The national planner can rationally choose zero quality R&D, not because the technology lacks private demand but because its incremental national benefit is outweighed by the real public cost and displaced market surplus. The mechanism is useful for diagnosing interregional public agricultural R&D coordination and evaluating quality programs separately from observed varietal adoption. It does not establish real-world prevalence or optimal abolition of breeding laboratories.
