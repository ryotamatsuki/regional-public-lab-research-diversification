In symmetric linear-demand interior, with h=1-t, j=b-c, e=b+n-2c, L=kappa+2h-e, xi=(2h-e)/(2L)>0 and a_star=(h-j)/(2h-e),

\[
G_{i,x_i}-W_{x_i}=\xi(a_{\rm star}-a).
\]

The sign change does **not** require research itself to move between strategic substitutes and complements (baseline cross-derivative -15/391 remains negative). The exact half-share special case relies on old/new demand symmetry b=n; not a universal real-world 50-percent prescription.

P2: under the paper's **restricted nonlinear demand specification** the sign instead follows H(1-a)-H(a), H(q)=q phi''(q); the same incidence-price calculus explains sign, vanishing, reversal. It is a **conditional mechanism robustness characterization**, not independent generic demand theory.

P3: exact globally verified regional best-response Nash with xD=389/6470 positive, socially optimal xP=0 and W_D-W_0=-2569157/418609000 at Delta=17/50. This is a rigorous *existence* example with small uncalibrated welfare loss, not a measured macroeconomic harm and not generic fresh proof that competing entities may overspend.

## 2. NEW checked policy interpretation is a corollary, not a fourth theorem

At an interior symmetric decentralized R&D Nash, G_{i,x_i}=0 and the national welfare derivative is

\[
\boxed{W_{x_i}(x_D,x_D)=-\xi(a_{\rm star}-a_D).}
\]

With interior strict planner and RM>1, the planner and Nash adoption shares sit on the same side of the threshold:
\[
a_D-a_{\rm star}=\frac{RM-1}{RM-1+\xi}(a_P-a_{\rm star});
\qquad
x_D-x_P=\frac{\xi M}{RM-1+\xi}(a_{\rm star}-a_P).
\]
Thus **observed decentralized** adoption a_D combined with *estimated* demand cross-substitution a_star can diagnose whether a *small unilateral* cut or increase in local research raises national welfare at the current equilibrium. This avoids assuming the planner's unobservable a_P is measured, but it is NOT a fully executable public-funding mechanism or an unconditional threshold. Must check interior, symmetry, price substitutes, the full demand coefficients and national welfare objective; symmetric-government simultaneous changes and corners require carefully stated limits. Exact SymPy checks and three old example calculations passed locally on 2026-10-24 in verify_policy_readout.py.

## 3. Strongest independent skeptical-editor rejection (subjective)

Remove agricultural labels. The mathematical core is a smooth two-leader linear-quadratic externality game; competitive downstream allocation is a quadratic KKT mapping, and local-minus-national research incentives are price-transfer incidence by the envelope theorem. The half-share threshold is generated by old/new demand symmetry; the nonlinear derivative is an immediate restricted-demand specialization of q times inverse-demand slope. Generic beggar-thy-neighbor investment competition, agricultural multi-product resource reallocation, geographically differentiated product development and even competitive acreage choice already occur in major published agricultural papers. There are no measured demand slopes, no credible quantitative economic loss, and no specified implementable funding-coordination rule. The three statements are not three distinct deep theoretical contributions. A full-length field research article would likely be judged insufficiently complete or original despite correct mathematics. No actual external human editor or referee has performed this review.

## 4. Editor-facing positive case and what is different

Public plant breeders improve product quality in a common downstream fruit market but act on territorial producer welfare. Endogenous growers' replanting produces a tractable adoption-share-dependent *sign* of the decentralized public R&D externality. The model and derivations pinpoint both possible research overinvestment and underinvestment, and show that the diagnostic pattern can be erased by changing the shape of inverse demand. A globally audited example illustrates mutually rational locally funded research that leaves national welfare below zero public research. This may be a self-contained **agricultural institutional application/interpretation** that would interest readers deciding how to assess regional spending when publicly bred varieties diffuse. It is not now an evidenced novel theorem against Alston–Freebairn–James 2001, Balagtas–Kim 2007, Lence et al 2007 or unpublished full-text Kinnucan–Miao 2000.
