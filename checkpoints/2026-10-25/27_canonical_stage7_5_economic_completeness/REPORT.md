## 2. Editor-facing substantive knowledge beyond mathematical correctness

**Single precise question:** Under what conditions can regionally funded public quality-improvement research be too high or too low from a national perspective even though competitive growers efficiently replace old varieties with better new cultivars, and can such excess be globally welfare-harmful without producer market power or fixed R&D entry fees?

**One central discovery, not three independent theories:**
The allocation of public research authority precedes and anticipates privately efficient constrained land conversion. Although the *downstream* farmer KKT maximizes welfare conditional on chosen quality, territorial lab incentives depend on how the induced product-price and consumer-surplus effects are allocated across regions. This gives a specific old/new share-dependent wedge. A precisely constructed no-fixed-fee, globally verified family has positive public quality investment as each laboratory's own best response but unique national optimal investment zero; at its symmetric equilibrium both regions strictly prefer coordinated zero effort to the mutual positive research profile even though unilateral cutback loses. In the analytic e=j=0 family, effort best responses do NOT respond to rivals (shared national CS still creates payoff interdependence); a distinct exact e=1/100 symmetric Nash exhibits genuinely nonzero strategic substitution but not all-asymmetric-Nash uniqueness.

**What a referee learns about the *institution*:**
- Successful private adoption is not evidence that the *preceding public research budget* is efficiently chosen.
- Whether a territorial public lab over- or underinvests relative to national welfare can depend on the OLD/NEW product composition, not on a constant tendency to overspend; this is conditional on symmetric interior solutions and globally established first-stage equilibria.
- A rigorous social-zero comparison is possible with ordinary convex continuous research cost and competitive growers, with a precise cutoff and excluded endpoint. It does not require inventing fixed entry costs or assigning growers monopoly power.
- Under the explicitly equal-old/new-curvature nonlinear-demand extension, the signed marginal incidence has a demand-curvature classification. An independent unequal-curvature counterexample falsifies unrestricted midpoint-neutrality. Thus demand shape/consumer incidence is a *hypothesis boundary*, not an all-market theorem.
- The government can rationally choose research for its territorial producer constituency despite a *mutual* preference for no public research in the model. This prisoner-dilemma/overpromotion pattern is already known as general economics; the concrete public-breeding interpretation and global demonstration are the application-specific result.

**No improper independent priority claims:**
Standard competitive welfare KKT, marginal-envelope arithmetic, generic rent-shifting/advertising investment, social-zero innovation, and resource reallocation in multi-market agriculture are **absorbed** by existing theory. See AFJ 2001, Balagtas and Kim 2007, Lence et al. 2007 and checkpoint 25. The exact threshold is not intrinsically a new universal lemma. This report does not infer absence of identical work from inaccessible published proofs.

## 3. Minimal causal chain and assumption criticality

The indispensable chain is:
\`regional public quality x_i\` -> \`grower best-response planting a_i(x)\` -> \`four-product prices and producer incomes\` -> \`territorial public G_i with own producer income + CS/2 - real research cost\` -> \`national W=sum G_i\` -> \`conditional efficiency but research-stage wedge and possible exact welfare reversal\`.

**Essential to exact baseline theorem:**
- Two public quality-choice leaders with nonnegative continuous x; distinct private price-taking growers who allocate fixed single-region land between old and new outputs; national common demand and *real* convex conversion/research costs.
- Public objective \`G_i=Pi_i+CS/2-C_i\`; national \`W=G_1+G_2\` without double counting nominal prices. CS/2 is **an assumption about regional valuation, not a fact about Japan**.
- Additive own-new-variety utility increment with specified demand B; positive definiteness/unique competitive adoption; exact feasibility and globally checked effort best-response and planner constraints.
- P1 signed crossing needs specified symmetric interior branch, \`xi>0\`, \`a_star\` in (0,1) for an admissible reversal, plus stricter conditions for actual xD vs xP ranking.
- P2's claim holds ONLY for identical old/new separable inverse-demand curvature in the specified symmetric interior N-region class; asymmetric curvature destroys half-share neutrality.
- P3+ interval \`zc=(-4+sqrt(13))/12 < z <=0\` and exact \`e=j=0\` family. At z=zc, FOUR Nash profiles, hence boundary excluded. The interacting e>0 exact example is separate, not an analytic all-e theorem.

**Facilitating restrictions/nontransferable scope:** unit land normalization, linear-quadratic demand and conversion/cost shape for exact closed forms; fixed old/new total region resources; no research uncertainty; static research/adoption order; no licensing/royalties in baseline, no emissions, time-to-maturity, yield/disease resistance or private R&D decision. These assumptions prevent broad transfer to Cournot/Bertrand industries or calibrated orchard policy.

**Actual preregistered falsification already completed:** quartic real R&D cost retains the qualitative harmful positive Nash (T1), unequal old/new curvature yields \`-1/16\` even at half adoption (T2), quartic conversion technology retains the specified P1 incidence sign \`7/151\` at tested point (T3); separate e=1/100 coupling example survives.
